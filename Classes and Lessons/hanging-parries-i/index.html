    Hanging Parries I

    How can I use an opponent's committed high cut to make a protected answer or leave safely, without remaining in a passive hanging parry?

    LESSON | INSTRUCTORS AUDIENCE

    Chris Mineweaser | July 2026 | Version 1.0

    ← All classes and lessons

    Class context: 120 MINUTES | MEYER WITH GLOSS SUPPORT

    The problem, behavior, and boundaries

    This is a longsword class for committed descending and diagonal cuts only. Students work both right- and left-side hanging-parry shapes, with modern padded jackets and clamshell gloves in mind. The lesson does not add thrusts, lower-line attacks, feint layers, or extended bind sequences.

    CENTRAL QUESTION: How can I use an opponent's committed high cut to make a protected answer or leave safely, without remaining in a passive hanging parry?

    END-OF-CLASS BEHAVIOR: Students recognize a catchable committed high or diagonal cut, receive it with an uncrowded two- handed structure that protects the head and body, then immediately either make a clean parry-riposte or leave at an angle under cover. They do not wait in the hanging parry.

    HISTORICAL ANCHOR: Joachim Meyer, Gründtliche Beschreibung der Kunst des Fechtens (1570), Part I, Chapter 22, 'Hanging' (fol. I.22r.3), with his Crosswise Cut (fol. I.12v.2); supported by the upper hangings in the Liechtenauer gloss tradition.

    Target-action success test

    The target behavior counts only when all four observable conditions are present.

    REQUIREMENT OBSERVABLE STANDARD
    Recognition The fencer uses the action against a genuinely committed high or diagonal cut, rather than trying to catch an attack that has already passed or a feint that has not committed.
    Reception The cut lands on a compact, connected blade structure: the head and body remain covered, the hands stay usable in gloves, and the defender does not collapse into a cramped crossed-arm position.
    Action Immediately after the catch, the defender either lands a clean riposte - commonly a zwerch or cross-cut - or moves out at an angle behind the sword.
    Outcome The defender neither accepts an afterblow nor remains hanging in place; a double or immediate return hit fails the lesson.

    NO DOUBLE: A result that leaves both fencers validly hit does not satisfy the lesson, even if one blade arrived first.

    Primary functional model

    Receive a committed upper-line cut on a raised, hanging blade with the hands and cross protecting the head. Use the attacker’s committed line to create time for one immediate answer, or use the blade as cover while stepping diagonally out. The parry is a transition, not a destination.

    Instructor hypothesis

    Students will often catch too late, leave the blade hanging after contact, over-compress the arms in clamshell gloves, or force a riposte after the attacker has recovered. Others will stop after landing their own first hit and eat the opponent's afterblow rather than use the hanging shape as exit cover.

    Preserve the sequence. If time is lost, compress explanation before removing open sparring or reflection.

    TRANSITION PLAN: Students gear before the opening. Pair by compatible control and size. Keep masks and gloves on from problem exposure through sparring. Use one audible timer and announce rotations before each interval ends.

    IF RUNNING LATE: Shorten the guided discussion or historical explanation by 2-3 minutes and reduce Game 2 by one role interval. Preserve at least one open-sparring round and the reflection.

    Arrival and orientation

    C | BLOCK 1 | 0:00-0:05

    Students understand the problem, success test, and class arc.

    OPENING SENTENCE: "Tonight we are solving one problem: How can I use an opponent's committed high cut to make a protected answer or leave safely, without remaining in a passive hanging parry?"

    SUCCESS DEFINITION: State the four-part success test in plain language and make clear that a double is a failed solution.

    CLASS ARC: Experience the problem first; compare what worked; examine one historical lens; increase choice and pressure; test transfer in open fencing.

    DO NOT: Teach the complete historical solution before the first game. Students should possess the problem before receiving the model.

    Athletic development

    C | BLOCK 2 | 0:05-0:20

    Reactive lateral stepping and compact upper-line coverage: receive force, change angle, and leave balance available for a cut or exit.

    WHAT THIS BLOCK IS: A short movement lesson, not conditioning. Demonstrate each exercise once, keep the volume low, and stop or regress when posture or landing quality deteriorates.

    TARGET QUALITY: Reactive lateral stepping and compact upper-line coverage: receive force, change angle, and leave balance available for a cut or exit.

    Exercise sequence

    EXERCISE DOSE COACHING FOCUS MOVEMENT PURPOSE
    Fencing stance pulse 2 x 20 seconds Quiet feet; level hips; hands ready to raise Prepare small weight shifts without telegraphing
    Diagonal load and stick 2 x 4 each side Land softly on an angled step; chest stays tall Build control for a protected diagonal exit
    Partner high-line cue 3 x 20 seconds each Raise the hands only on the actual cue; keep elbows usable Link visual information to compact upper-line cover
    Cover, angle, reset 2 x 5 each side Cover the head, take one diagonal step, finish balanced Rehearse leaving immediately instead of freezing in the cover

    How to perform the exercises

    1. FENCING STANCE PULSE

    Stand in a comfortable fencing stance without a sword. Make tiny forward-back weight shifts with the feet in contact with the floor. On an irregular clap, freeze instantly in a balanced stance with the hands ready to rise. Do not bounce or let the head drift. Cue: 'Light feet; quiet eyes.'

    2. DIAGONAL LOAD AND STICK

    From fencing stance, take one short diagonal step forward and slightly outward, then settle into a stable athletic position for two seconds. Repeat to both sides. The step is not a leap; the knee tracks over the foot and the chest stays tall. Cue: 'Angle, land, own the stop.'

    3. PARTNER HIGH-LINE CUE

    Partners face each other without swords. The feeder gives harmless shoulder or hand noise, then occasionally raises one hand toward the mover's upper line. Only on the real cue, the mover raises both hands into a compact head-cover shape without crushing the forearms together. Reset immediately. Cue: 'See the line; make room for your hands.'

    4. COVER, ANGLE, RESET

    Start in fencing stance with the hands low. On a partner's high-line cue, bring the hands to a compact head-cover position and take one diagonal step away from the cue. Freeze balanced for one second, then return to guard. Alternate sides. Cue: 'Cover, angle, reset - do not park there.'

    REGRESSION AND STOP RULE: Reduce speed, distance, or impact; replace jumps with controlled steps. Stop for pain, dizziness, repeated loss of balance, loud uncontrolled landings, or a student who cannot maintain the described posture.

    Representative problem exposure: Catch and Clear

    C | BLOCK 3 | 0:20-0:40

    Let students discover when a high or diagonal attack is actually catchable, and make an immediate exit the non-negotiable end of the exchange.

    STARTING STATE: In a narrow lane, partners begin at a distance where one controlled advance can bring the attacker into high-cut range. Attacker delivers one committed descending or diagonal cut to either upper opening. Defender begins from a neutral, mobile guard.

    PLAYER GOALS: Defender recognizes the line, receives it on either hanging-parry side with safe structure, and exits diagonally under cover before the attacker can make a valid return.

    RULES AND CONSTRAINTS: Attacker may choose either upper-side cut and may take one controlled return only if the defender remains available. Defender may not stand in a hanging parry before the attack begins and may not remain in it after contact. No riposte is required in this first game.

    SCORING: Correct catch plus clearly covered angled exit 2; clean exit without a full catch 1; attacker clean hit 1; passive hang, late catch, afterblow, or double 0-0.

    DOSE AND ROTATION: 4 minutes each role; change partner; repeat. Use the last 2 minutes for a short room debrief inside the 20-minute block.

    INSTRUCTOR OBSERVES: Early waiting in the hanging shape, catching too late, hands jammed together by gloves, head outside the protection, square exits, and defenders admiring the parry instead of leaving.

    COACH POSTURE: Observe across the room before intervening. Record common patterns and let several exchanges occur before deciding what the class needs.

    Guided coaching

    C | BLOCK 4 | 0:40-0:55

    Use the first game's evidence to provide the smallest intervention that improves the class.

    Debrief questions

    1. What told you the cut was committed enough to catch?

    2. Where did your head sit relative to your hands, cross, and blade?

    3. Could you still use both hands freely in your gloves after the catch?

    4. What changed when you stepped out at an angle instead of backward or straight in?

    PRIMARY INTERVENTION: Show a compact right- and left-side hanging receipt against a committed diagonal high cut. Keep the hands apart enough to work in clamshell gloves, get the head behind the sword, and let the feet make the parry functional. Give one cue: 'Catch, answer or angle out.' Demonstrate a common passive hang and why it loses the afterblow.

    VARIABLE REHEARSAL: Partners re-test the target problem at approximately 60-70% intensity. Keep at least one meaningful variable - side, rhythm, distance, pressure, or response - rather than feeding identical repetitions.

    FEEDBACK PLAN: Use one cue at a time. Correct immediate danger at once; otherwise give feedback between sets of three attempts. Ask the student to identify the information that shaped the action.

    AVOID: Turning the block into a long lecture or adding several fixes before students can test the first one.

    Historical integration: Meyer With Gloss Support

    C | BLOCK 5 | 0:55-1:05

    Connect the experienced tactical problem to a documented historical principle without claiming that the source prescribes the modern game.

    SOURCE IDENTIFICATION: Joachim Meyer, Gründtliche Beschreibung der Kunst des Fechtens (1570), Part I, Chapter 22, 'Hanging' (fol. I.22r.3), with his Crosswise Cut (fol. I.12v.2); supported by the upper hangings in the Liechtenauer gloss tradition.

    VERIFIED HISTORICAL CLAIM: Meyer describes 'Hanging' from Plow as lifting the hilt so the blade hangs somewhat downward, receiving the opponent's cut on the flat of the blade, then working with winding to the nearest opening. His Crosswise Cut is likewise described as countering an upper cut while maintaining the crossguard high over the head and striking at the same time. [1] [2]

    MODERN TRAINING INTERPRETATION: Use Meyer's hanging description to show the functional idea of a protected reception that becomes work, and his Zwerch to show an answer that covers while striking. The exact tournament objective here - score, cover the likely return, and take an angled exit - is a modern coaching application. The lesson does not require students to reproduce any cramped crossed-arm shape that is unreliable in padded jackets and clamshell gloves.

    DEMONSTRATION: Show three slow examples: one successful application, one common failure, and one case in which the action should be declined. Let students rehearse briefly, then return to live choice.

    EVIDENCE LINE: Historical text supplies the cited principle or action. The success test, games, scoring, cues, and safety adaptations are modern coaching design.

    Progressive constraints

    C | BLOCK 6 | 1:05-1:30

    Increase uncertainty while preserving the lesson's central information-action relationship.

    Game 2 - Catch, Answer, or Angle | 12 minutes

    Make the defender choose between a clean parry-riposte and a protected exit instead of forcing a preselected follow-up.

    • Partners begin at high-cut range. Attacker gives one committed high or diagonal cut to either side, then may recover and defend normally. Defender begins mobile and may use either side of the hanging parry.

    • Defender catches safely and chooses the best immediate resolution: zwerch/cross-cut riposte if the opening remains, or angled exit if the attacker has recovered or distance closes poorly.

    • One defensive reception, then one immediate answer or exit. A riposte must arrive while the defender remains covered; no second chase. Attacker may make one controlled afterblow against a passive or uncovered defender.

    • Clean catch, clean riposte, and no return 2; clean catch and protected exit 2; clean riposte that accepts an afterblow 0; attacker clean hit 1; double 0-0.

    • 5 minutes each role; 2 minutes to explain the change in scoring and transition to the next game.

    Game 3 - Hit, Cover, Leave | 13 minutes

    Train the user’s primary competition application: after landing or threatening a score, use a hanging- parry shape as cover for the opponent’s committed return and leave on your terms.

    • Initiator begins from just outside measure and makes one controlled scoring attack to an upper opening. Responder then gives one committed high or diagonal return at the initiator's upper line. The initiator must recognize the return, cover with the appropriate hanging-parry side, and leave at an angle; roles alternate every exchange.

    • Initiator lands or credibly threatens the first action, then turns the opponent's committed return into cover for a safe angled departure. The responder learns to punish a stationary, uncovered finish.

    • The initial action can be any safe upper-line attack. The responder's return must be a real committed high/diagonal cut, not a pre-frozen feed. The initiator may riposte only if it is immediately available; otherwise leaving is the correct answer. Halt once safe separation is established.

    • Clean initial score or credible threat plus covered angled exit 2; cover plus clean immediate riposte plus exit 3; initial score followed by a valid return hit 0; double 0-0.

    • Rotate initiator/responder every 3 minutes. Use the final minute to remove the announced return and prepare for open fencing.

    PROGRESSION TRIGGER: Remove the announced first attacker; either fencer may score first, and the scorer must solve the first committed upper-line return that follows.

    REGRESSION TRIGGER: Keep the initiator designated and the return side known for one set, then restore side choice before moving to open sparring.

    Open sparring

    C | BLOCK 7 | 1:30-1:55

    Test whether the behavior appears without forced actions or bonus scoring.

    FORMAT: Five 4-minute rounds with approximately 1 minute between rounds for rotation and brief feedback. Use normal open-fencing rules and the club's usual intensity agreement.

    SHARED OBJECTIVE: "Fence normally. Use hanging parries only against catchable committed high or diagonal attacks. If you use one, either make the answer immediately or leave at an angle; do not remain hanging in range."

    PAIRING PLAN: Use compatible control and size, then rotate across different rhythms and preferences. Supervise novice pairings and avoid a large mismatch solely to complete the rotation.

    Coach records

    • Selects committed upper-line attacks rather than late or speculative catches
    • Head protected; flat/strong receives; hands remain usable in gloves
    • Can receive left and right without defaulting to one unsafe shape
    • Moves while the opponent is committed, not after the hit
    • Ripostes only when opening remains and stays covered
    • Changes angle with the sword covering the line
    • Prevents the return and avoids doubles
    • Uses the action selectively in open fencing

    FEEDBACK: Observe during rounds and coach mainly between them. Open sparring is not another constrained game.

    Reflection and close

    C | BLOCK 8 | 1:55-2:00

    Students consolidate one usable cue and one testable next action.

    Whole-group questions

    1. Which opponent action made the hanging parry a good choice - and which did not?

    2. When did a zwerch or cross-cut remain clean, and when was exit the smarter answer?

    3. How did your jacket and gloves change the shape you could actually use?

    4. What did you do after a successful score to prevent the return?

    INDIVIDUAL NEXT ACTION: Each student states one small, observable action they will test the next time this problem appears.

    INSTRUCTOR CLOSE: Restate the functional success test, not a technique name. Record any safety issue, equipment problem, or student who needs a lower-intensity follow-up.

    END ON TIME: Do not let the final sparring round consume reflection. The class is incomplete until students name the information that shaped their decision.

    Catch and Clear

    GAME CARD 1

    Let students discover when a high or diagonal attack is actually catchable, and make an immediate exit the non-negotiable end of the exchange.

    STARTING STATE: In a narrow lane, partners begin at a distance where one controlled advance can bring the attacker into high-cut range. Attacker delivers one committed descending or diagonal cut to either upper opening. Defender begins from a neutral, mobile guard.

    PLAYER GOALS: Defender recognizes the line, receives it on either hanging-parry side with safe structure, and exits diagonally under cover before the attacker can make a valid return.

    RULES AND CONSTRAINTS: Attacker may choose either upper-side cut and may take one controlled return only if the defender remains available. Defender may not stand in a hanging parry before the attack begins and may not remain in it after contact. No riposte is required in this first game.

    SCORING: Correct catch plus clearly covered angled exit 2; clean exit without a full catch 1; attacker clean hit 1; passive hang, late catch, afterblow, or double 0-0.

    DOSE AND ROTATION: 4 minutes each role; change partner; repeat. Use the last 2 minutes for a short room debrief inside the 20-minute block.

    COACH OBSERVES: Early waiting in the hanging shape, catching too late, hands jammed together by gloves, head outside the protection, square exits, and defenders admiring the parry instead of leaving.

    PROGRESSION: Let the attacker vary onset rhythm and choose either upper opening without signaling.

    REGRESSION: Use one known attack side, slow the delivery, and have the attacker hold the sword extended after the cut so the defender can rehearse the exit line.

    Catch, Answer, or Angle

    GAME CARD 2

    Make the defender choose between a clean parry-riposte and a protected exit instead of forcing a preselected follow-up.

    STARTING STATE: Partners begin at high-cut range. Attacker gives one committed high or diagonal cut to either side, then may recover and defend normally. Defender begins mobile and may use either side of the hanging parry.

    PLAYER GOALS: Defender catches safely and chooses the best immediate resolution: zwerch/cross-cut riposte if the opening remains, or angled exit if the attacker has recovered or distance closes poorly.

    RULES AND CONSTRAINTS: One defensive reception, then one immediate answer or exit. A riposte must arrive while the defender remains covered; no second chase. Attacker may make one controlled afterblow against a passive or uncovered defender.

    SCORING: Clean catch, clean riposte, and no return 2; clean catch and protected exit 2; clean riposte that accepts an afterblow 0; attacker clean hit 1; double 0-0.

    DOSE AND ROTATION: 5 minutes each role; 2 minutes to explain the change in scoring and transition to the next game.

    COACH OBSERVES: Whether the student sees the real opening, forces the zwerch after recovery, fails to move the feet, or exits with the weapon no longer between the bodies.

    PROGRESSION: Allow the attacker to choose a shallow recovery, full recovery, or immediate controlled afterblow after the cut.

    REGRESSION: Have the attacker hold their recovery for one beat. Defenders name 'answer' or 'exit' aloud before acting, then remove the call as reading improves.

    Hit, Cover, Leave

    GAME CARD 3

    Train the user’s primary competition application: after landing or threatening a score, use a hanging-parry shape as cover for the opponent’s committed return and leave on your terms.

    STARTING STATE: Initiator begins from just outside measure and makes one controlled scoring attack to an upper opening. Responder then gives one committed high or diagonal return at the initiator's upper line. The initiator must recognize the return, cover with the appropriate hanging-parry side, and leave at an angle; roles alternate every exchange.

    PLAYER GOALS: Initiator lands or credibly threatens the first action, then turns the opponent's committed return into cover for a safe angled departure. The responder learns to punish a stationary, uncovered finish.

    RULES AND CONSTRAINTS: The initial action can be any safe upper-line attack. The responder's return must be a real committed high/diagonal cut, not a pre-frozen feed. The initiator may riposte only if it is immediately available; otherwise leaving is the correct answer. Halt once safe separation is established.

    SCORING: Clean initial score or credible threat plus covered angled exit 2; cover plus clean immediate riposte plus exit 3; initial score followed by a valid return hit 0; double 0-0.

    DOSE AND ROTATION: Rotate initiator/responder every 3 minutes. Use the final minute to remove the announced return and prepare for open fencing.

    COACH OBSERVES: Whether the first attacker stops after scoring, whether the return is actually committed, whether the cover arrives on the correct side, and whether the exit changes angle rather than simply backing away in the same line.

    PROGRESSION: Remove the announced first attacker; either fencer may score first, and the scorer must solve the first committed upper-line return that follows.

    REGRESSION: Keep the initiator designated and the return side known for one set, then restore side choice before moving to open sparring.

    Plan modifications before the room forces them

    Preserve the information and decision while reducing uncertainty, speed, distance, or impact.

    CONDITION PLANNED RESPONSE
    First-time student Use a known attack or response, longer preparation time, controlled partner, and score safe resolution as success.
    Mixed experience Give beginners less uncertainty; give experienced students more choice or rhythm variation, not greater impact.
    Odd attendance Use attacker, defender, and observer. Observer applies the four-part success test, then roles rotate.
    Limited space Use narrow lanes; prohibit passing and large leaps; emphasize compact footwork and controlled stops.
    Injury / low impact Replace bounds with steps; reduce speed and contact; permit observer or limited-role participation.
    Large attendance Create lanes, assign a senior student to each cluster, and synchronize changes with one timer.
    Game breaks down Remove one variable, increase distance, slow the action, or return to a known starting state.
    Time loss Compress explanation and one role interval; preserve open transfer and reflection.

    What to watch without interrupting every exchange

    Use the checklist to distinguish learning from mere scoring.

    DOMAIN LOOK FOR COMMON FAILURE
    Recognition Selects committed upper-line attacks rather than late or speculative catches Tries to catch every attack or arrives after the line has passed
    Structure Head protected; flat/strong receives; hands remain usable in gloves Cramps/crosses arms, loses head cover, or collapses into weak contact
    Both sides Can receive left and right without defaulting to one unsafe shape Only recognizes or reaches one side
    Timing Moves while the opponent is committed, not after the hit Waits in the parry or catches late
    Answer Ripostes only when opening remains and stays covered Forces a zwerch/cross-cut into recovery
    Exit Changes angle with the sword covering the line Backs straight up, stops in range, or lowers the sword
    Outcome Prevents the return and avoids doubles Counts the first score despite afterblow or double
    Transfer Uses the action selectively in open fencing Performs only after an announced feed

    Between-round feedback formula

    Observation -> question -> one cue -> re-test. Name what happened, ask what information the student perceived, offer one change, and let the next round test it.

    Class-level success evidence

    • Cleaner solutions and fewer doubles from Game 1 to Game 3.
    • Students can name at least one reliable cue.
    • The target behavior appears selectively in open fencing.
    • Students can explain why an unsafe score does not satisfy the lesson.
    • Beginners show safer structure even when they do not score.
