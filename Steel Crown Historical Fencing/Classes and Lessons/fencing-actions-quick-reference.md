# Fencing Actions Quick Reference

[← All classes and lessons](index.md)

| TERM | WHAT IT MEANS | LONGSWORD EXAMPLE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attack | The initial offensive action intended to hit. | Step in and cut to the head. |
| Counterattack | An attack made into the opponent's attack, without first completing a parry. | Strike the hands while their cut is developing. |
| Stop hit | A counterattack intended to land before the opponent completes their attack. | Thrust during a large preparation. |
| Parry-riposte | First stop or displace the attack; then immediately attack back. | Parry the descending cut, then return to the head. |
| Attack in opposition | Attack while simultaneously controlling or displacing the opposing weapon. | Thrust while carrying their blade safely off line. |
| Compound attack | An offensive sequence using one or more feints before the final attack. | Feint high, draw the parry, then cut underneath. |
| Second intention | Plan the first action to provoke a predicted response that the prepared second action defeats. | Attack high expecting the parry, then redirect below it. |
| Remise | Immediately renew the attack in the same line without fully recovering. | A parried thrust stays forward and thrusts again. |
| Redoublement | Immediately renew the attack in a new line without fully returning to guard. | A head cut is parried; redirect to the other side. |
| Reprise | Begin a new attack after recovering or returning to guard. | Recover from the failed attack, then launch again. |
| Countertime | Draw or anticipate a counterattack, defeat it, then finish your own offense. | Invite the hand shot, parry it, then riposte. |
| Afterblow | A retaliatory hit delivered shortly after being struck; especially important in HEMA rulesets. | Your head hit lands, but their immediate return hits your arm. |

## The fast distinction

Counterattack: hit during their attack. Parry-riposte: defend, then hit. Compound attack: feint within the attack. Second intention: use the first action to deliberately create the response defeated by the second.

Terminology varies somewhat by fencing tradition and ruleset. In foil and sabre, several labels also carry right-of-way consequences; most HEMA rulesets use the tactical ideas without applying Olympic priority exactly.
