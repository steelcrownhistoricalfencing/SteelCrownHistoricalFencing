# Compact Cutting II

[← All classes and lessons](index.md)

**Class context:** 120 MINUTES \| MEYER HISTORICAL LENS

## Make the next decision before the moment disappears

**LESSON OVERVIEW**

Part I built useful endpoints. Part II tests whether the fencer can read the opponent's real response and continue only while continuing remains safe.

**CENTRAL QUESTION:** After my first action, can I immediately recognize whether to cut again, defend, follow, or leave?
{: #central-question}

**WORKING PRINCIPLE:** Fast enough to exploit the opening. Controlled enough to abandon the chain.
{: #working-principle}

**STUDENT CUE:** Finish. Read. Continue or protect.
{: #student-cue}

**END-OF-CLASS BEHAVIOR:** The fencer makes a credible compact first cut, recognizes whether the blade is free or controlled, and chooses an immediate follow-up, defensive action, pursuit, or exit without pausing to reset the weapon.
{: #end-of-class-behavior}

**PART I CONNECTION:** Use the position the first action created: crossed or uncrossed hands, point near the target, and hips still available. Do not withdraw simply to recreate a familiar guard.
{: #part-i-connection}

**COMMON NON-EXAMPLE:** Three fast cuts thrown because three cuts were planned. Speed does not rescue an invented opening, a blade already under control, or a follow-up that produces a double.
{: #common-non-example}

**EXPERIMENTAL STANDARD:** The class is testing which endpoints make rapid decisions possible. Keep the mechanics that remain credible under pressure and discard the ones that require the defender to cooperate.
{: #experimental-standard}

## Spring, brake, and redirect

**WARM-UP \| 0:00-0:15**

Repeat the approved plyometric sequence. The goal is elastic movement, clean braking, and the ability to change direction without losing posture.

| TIME | MOVEMENT | COACHING FOCUS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00-0:05 | Pogo hops in place, then small forward, backward, and lateral hops | Stay tall; spring through the ankles; land quietly; stop while quality is high |
| 0:05-0:10 | Low boxes: two-foot on and off, alternating single-foot, then lateral on and off | Use a height everyone owns; knees track cleanly; stabilize before redirecting |
| 0:10-0:15 | Short sprints of 5-10 meters, controlled braking, then a slow walk back | Sharp first steps; balanced deceleration; the return walk is recovery |

**WHY THIS WARM-UP:** Rapid close work depends on acceleration that can be stopped or redirected. The athlete must be able to change the decision without the body continuing blindly in the old direction.
{: #why-this-warm-up}

**ADAPTATION:** Use steps instead of jumps for anyone managing impact or balance limitations. Lower the box before sacrificing landing control, and shorten sprint lanes if braking becomes sloppy.
{: #adaptation}

**TRANSITION:** Mask and glove up. Begin with controlled endpoints before adding pressure or choice.
{: #transition}

## Read the position the first cut created

**TECHNICAL REVIEW \| 0:15-0:30**

Review Part I quickly, then attach one clear decision to each common outcome. The purpose is recognition, not rehearsing a long combination.

| AFTER THE FIRST ACTION | USEFUL FIRST DECISION | WARNING |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A new line is open and the sword is free | Cut directly from the endpoint | Do not withdraw to reload |
| The blade is caught or the return is already coming | Cover, counter-parry, disengage, or leave | Do not force a second cut through control |
| The opponent retreats but measure remains | Follow with the feet and keep the cut compact | Do not chase with the arms alone |
| The opponent crashes closer or the hands collapse | Create working space, cover, or separate | Do not windmill from a trapped position |

**PARTNER EXPLORATION:** In full gear at drilling intensity, make one compact cut and freeze at its endpoint. The partner gives one of the four outcomes above. Resume for one decision only, then reset. Change roles every three repetitions.
{: #partner-exploration}

**MECHANICS TO REVISIT:** Crossed hands may uncross into a Zwerch-like route. Uncrossed hands may use a modest hip and torso turn to change line. Both are options, not automatic answers.
{: #mechanics-to-revisit}

**CONTACT STANDARD:** In HEMA, we cut at things, not through things. Reach the target with a legible cutting plane, structure, and control, then use the endpoint instead of manufacturing extra follow-through.
{: #contact-standard}

## Move quickly without inventing the next opening

**MEYER SOURCE STUDY \| 0:30-0:45**

Meyer supports prompt movement from one opening to another. The classroom problem is deciding whether that next opening actually exists under pressure.

**MEYER, 1570:** As soon as you have touched, do not tarry any longer, but instead let it fly quickly away from one opening to another.

**WHAT THE SOURCE SUPPORTS:** After touching or making contact, the sword should not remain idle. Meyer repeatedly uses connected actions, opening-to-opening attacks, crossed-hand positions, and Zwerch actions in constrained lines.
{: #what-the-source-supports}

**WHAT PART II ADDS:** Modern training can isolate the perception beneath that flow: Is the blade free? Did a new target appear? Is a return already arriving? Did distance expand or collapse?
{: #what-part-ii-adds}

| HISTORICAL CLUE | PART II TRAINING QUESTION |
| --- | --- |
| Do not tarry after touching | Can the fencer recognize the next decision without pausing to reset? |
| Move from one opening to another | Did the defender actually create a new opening? |
| Zwerch and body-supported actions work in constrained lines | Can the fencer change line while staying able to stop, cover, or leave? |

**INTERPRETIVE LIMIT:** This is not evidence for a mandatory three-cut chain. Meyer provides historical direction; the game's free-or-controlled distinction and decision branches are modern coaching tools.
{: #interpretive-limit}

**SOURCE NOTE:** Joachim Meyer, Gründtliche Beschreibung der Kunst des Fechtens (1570), Longsword, fol. I.55r; English translation by Rebecca L. R. Garber and transcription by Michael Chidester, via Wiktenauer.
{: #source-note}

## Free or Controlled

**GAME 1 \| 0:45-1:05**

Make the fastest useful distinction in close fencing: continue when the sword is free, and protect yourself when the opponent has gained control or begun the return.

**WHY THIS GAME NOW:** Part I taught students to recycle an endpoint. This game prevents that useful habit from becoming an automatic second cut.
{: #why-this-game-now}

**STARTING STATE:** Full gear. Begin at close striking measure. The attacker makes one known compact cut to an upper opening. The defender chooses between two coached answers: a cover that leaves the attacker's blade free and another line open, or a controlling parry with an immediate measured riposte.
{: #starting-state}

**ATTACKER:** If the blade remains free and a target appears, make one immediate compact follow-up. If the blade is controlled or the riposte begins, abandon the chain and defend, disengage, or leave.
{: #attacker}

**DEFENDER:** Make both answers honest but readable. A free cover should stop the first target without pretending to own the blade. A controlling parry should close the line and begin a safe return without waiting for permission.
{: #defender}

**STUDENT RULES:** Maximum two attacks. A follow-up through clear control fails even if it lands. A successful defense or exit is a correct answer, not a lost attacking turn. Reset on a clean hit, double, firm control, or separation.
{: #student-rules}

**DOSE AND ROTATION:** Use 90-second roles, then switch. Change partners after both roles. Begin with the two answers alternating, then let the defender choose freely.
{: #dose-and-rotation}

**COACH WATCHES:** Whether the attacker reads pressure and the beginning of the riposte rather than merely noticing blade contact; whether the follow-up begins without a withdrawal; and whether stopping remains possible.
{: #coach-watches}

**IF THE ROOM IS READY:** Let the defender vary the firmness and timing of contact so the distinction becomes less visually obvious.
{: #if-the-room-is-ready}

**IF THE ROOM NEEDS LESS:** Announce 'free' or 'controlled' after the parry and slow the exchange until the attacker's choice consistently matches the state.
{: #if-the-room-needs-less}

## Follow the Opening

**GAME 2 \| 1:05-1:25**

Chain compact attacks only as long as the defender keeps creating a real new line and the attacker's sword remains available.

**WHY THIS GAME NOW:** Game 1 established the stop condition. Now the defender can close successive openings, allowing the attacker to practice rapid crossed-to-uncrossed and hip-driven changes without memorizing the chain length.
{: #why-this-game-now_1}

**STARTING STATE:** Full gear. Begin at close measure. The attacker launches one credible compact cut. The defender may leave it open, cover once, or cover twice. Covers may redirect the blade but should not deliberately trap it during the first acquisition rounds.
{: #starting-state_1}

**ATTACKER:** Finish immediately if the current target remains open. Change line only when an actual cover removes it. Use the endpoint already reached and stop the chain if structure, measure, or blade freedom disappears.
{: #attacker_1}

**DEFENDER:** Make each cover late enough that the preceding threat was real. Sometimes stop after zero or one cover so the attacker cannot safely perform a memorized three-action sequence.
{: #defender_1}

**STUDENT RULES:** The attacker may change line no more than twice: three credible threats total. Every continuation must answer a defense that occurred. The phrase ends on a hit, double, clear blade control, loss of balance, or full separation.
{: #student-rules_1}

**DOSE AND ROTATION:** Use 90-second roles and change partners after both roles. Give many short phrases rather than one prolonged exchange.
{: #dose-and-rotation_1}

**COACH WATCHES:** A first threat that genuinely demands defense; compact travel between targets; an immediate finish when no new cover appears; and the ability to stop before the third action when the situation changes.
{: #coach-watches_1}

**IF THE ROOM IS READY:** Allow a controlling parry or immediate counterattack at any point. The attacker must protect instead of completing the planned chain.
{: #if-the-room-is-ready_1}

**IF THE ROOM NEEDS LESS:** Use one known three-threat route for several repetitions, then restore the defender's ability to stop covering after the first or second threat.
{: #if-the-room-needs-less_1}

## Continue, Cover, or Leave

**GAME 3 \| 1:25-1:40**

Apply compact follow-ups inside a short live phrase where the opponent may cover, counterattack, retreat, crash closer, or establish control.

**WHY THIS GAME NOW:** The first two games isolated blade freedom and opening recognition. This game adds the changing measure and immediate danger that determine whether rapid follow-ups survive in fencing.
{: #why-this-game-now_2}

**STARTING STATE:** Full gear. Begin just outside close striking measure. The designated attacker enters with one credible action. After it launches, both fencers may move and answer normally within the short-phrase limits.
{: #starting-state_2}

**ATTACKER:** Use the first endpoint to choose one response: cut a real opening, cover the return, follow a manageable retreat, create space from a crash, or leave. Continue only while the blade, balance, and target all support it.
{: #attacker_2}

**DEFENDER:** Defend naturally. You may cover, riposte, retreat, step closer, or establish blade control. Punish blind continuation with controlled contact, but do not turn the game into prolonged wrestling.
{: #defender_2}

**STUDENT RULES:** The designated attacker gets no more than three attacking actions. Either fencer may end the phrase sooner with a clean hit, clear control, safe separation, or a double. Reset immediately and preserve decision volume.
{: #student-rules_2}

**DOSE AND ROTATION:** Use 90-second roles and rotate partners after both roles. Change starting guards and first actions so the same endpoint does not solve every phrase.
{: #dose-and-rotation_2}

**COACH WATCHES:** Whether the attacker recognizes when the problem has changed from offense to defense; whether pursuit uses the feet; whether close pressure collapses the hands; and whether exits remain balanced and covered.
{: #coach-watches_2}

**IF THE ROOM IS READY:** Remove the designated attacker and let either fencer recognize the close-measure moment, while retaining the three-attack cap.
{: #if-the-room-is-ready_2}

**IF THE ROOM NEEDS LESS:** Limit the defender to three announced possibilities: cover and open a line, controlling parry and riposte, or retreat.
{: #if-the-room-needs-less_2}

## Transfer without forcing the chain

**OPEN SPARRING \| 1:40-2:00**

Return to normal fencing. The goal is not to produce more cuts; it is to make a better decision sooner when the exchange compresses.

**FORMAT:** Fence normal Steel Crown rounds of 3-4 minutes and rotate partners. Use ordinary scoring and intensity agreements. Give no bonus points for compact cutting or multi-hit sequences.
{: #format}

**SHARED OBJECTIVE:** When distance closes, recognize the first useful endpoint. Continue only if the blade is free, the opening is real, and the next action does not ignore an immediate return.
{: #shared-objective}

**COACH OBSERVES:** Whether the first attack was credible; whether a follow-up was caused by the defender; whether the fencer notices control before striking through it; and whether retreats, crashes, and counterattacks change the decision.
{: #coach-observes}

**FEEDBACK RHYTHM:** Watch several complete phrases before intervening. Between rounds, use one short question: What told you to continue, and what told you to stop?
{: #feedback-rhythm}

**TRANSFER CUE:** Finish. Read. Continue or protect.
{: #transfer-cue}

**OPTIONAL CLOSE:** Ask each pair for one endpoint that supported a clean follow-up and one situation where abandoning the chain was clearly better.
{: #optional-close}

## Observation and troubleshooting

**COACH REFERENCE**

Protect the lesson's real outcome: rapid decisions from compact positions, not rapid cutting for its own sake.

| IF YOU SEE... | TRY... |
| --- | --- |
| Every phrase becomes three cuts | Have the defender sometimes leave the first target open or gain control immediately |
| Attacker continues through the riposte | Make the return immediate and reward the counter-parry, cover, or exit as the correct solution |
| Follow-ups require a large reload | Freeze the first endpoint and find the shortest credible route before restoring speed |
| Retreat is chased with reaching arms | Require the feet to recover measure before the blade continues |
| Hands collapse during a crash | Slow the entry, preserve working space, and make separation a successful answer |
| Students stop after every first cut | Use a cooperative cover that clearly exposes the next line, then restore choice |

**QUESTIONS THAT PRESERVE DISCOVERY:** Was the sword free or controlled? What real opening caused the next cut? Did the opponent's feet change measure? When did the exchange become defensive? Could the fencer have abandoned the chain one action sooner?
{: #questions-that-preserve-discovery}

**RELATION TO EARLIER LESSONS:** Compound Attacks changes line because a defense is forming before blade control. Second Intention prepares for the opponent's answering action after the defense. Compact Cutting II begins from the compressed endpoint and asks which of those problems - or an exit - now exists.
{: #relation-to-earlier-lessons}

**RECORD AFTER CLASS:** Note which endpoints survived live pressure, which cues helped students recognize control, how often follow-ups became doubles, and whether the three-action cap improved or distorted decisions.
{: #record-after-class}

**ADAPTATIONS:** Newer fencers receive named responses and fixed targets. Advanced fencers vary pressure, timing, measure, and whether the expected line appears. Odd groups use an observer who names the endpoint, opponent response, and chosen decision.
{: #adaptations}

## Notes from the class

Record what remained credible under pressure, where students lost the ability to stop, and what should change before teaching the block again.
