# Compact Cutting I

[← All classes and lessons](index.md)

**Class context:** 120 MINUTES \| MEYER HISTORICAL LENS

## Make the next threat from where the sword finishes

**LESSON OVERVIEW**

Compact cutting is the skill of finishing one credible cut in a position that can immediately threaten, cover, redirect, or leave.

**CENTRAL QUESTION:** How can I keep producing credible threats at close measure without withdrawing the sword to reset?
{: #central-question}

**WORKING PRINCIPLE:** Cut at the target. Finish useful. Attack the next opening from where you already are.
{: #working-principle}

**STUDENT CUE:** Cut. Finish useful. Read the opening. Recycle or leave.
{: #student-cue}

**END-OF-CLASS BEHAVIOR:** The first cut has edge alignment, a legible cutting plane, and body support. Any follow-up begins from the real endpoint, answers an opening or pressure that actually appeared, and leaves the fencer able to stop, cover, or exit.
{: #end-of-class-behavior}

**COMMON NON-EXAMPLE:** A loose wrist tap, a large withdrawal to reload, a memorized Zwerch chain thrown regardless of the defender, or a frantic sequence that ends in a double.
{: #common-non-example}

**RESEARCH POSTURE:** This is an experimental acquisition lesson. Meyer supplies historical clues; the crossed-hand, uncrossed- hand, and hip-turn mechanics are modern hypotheses to test rather than settled universal answers.
{: #research-posture}

## Spring, brake, and redirect

**WARM-UP \| 0:00-0:15**

Prepare elastic feet, quiet landings, quick changes of direction, and recovery without stiffness. This is preparation, not conditioning.

| TIME | MOVEMENT | COACHING FOCUS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00-0:05 | Pogo hops in place, then small forward, backward, and lateral hops | Stay tall; spring through the ankles; land quietly; stop while quality is high |
| 0:05-0:10 | Low boxes: two-foot on and off, alternating single-foot, then lateral on and off | Use a height everyone owns; knees track cleanly; stabilize before changing direction |
| 0:10-0:15 | Short sprints of 5-10 meters, controlled braking, then a slow walk back | Sharp first steps; balanced deceleration; the return walk is recovery |

**WHY THIS WARM-UP:** Compact cutting uses the same broad rhythm: accelerate, arrest motion without collapsing, and redirect from the position actually reached. The warm-up previews that quality without pretending to reproduce sword mechanics.
{: #why-this-warm-up}

**ADAPTATION:** Use steps instead of jumps for anyone managing impact or balance limitations. Reduce the box height before reducing control, and keep sprint lanes short enough that braking remains clean.
{: #adaptation}

**TRANSITION:** Mask and glove up and move directly into controlled cutting before the room cools down.
{: #transition}

## What makes a compact cut matter

**TECHNICAL EXPLORATION \| 0:15-0:30**

Show the contrast, then let students feel it. The goal is a shorter reusable action, not the smallest possible action and not one mandatory body mechanic.

| EXAMPLE | WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE | WHAT IT TEACHES |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Large reset | The sword withdraws or loops before every new line | Credible, but too much dead travel for the available time |
| Loose tap | The hands flick while edge and body connection disappear | Short is not automatically meaningful contact |
| Compact cut | The blade reaches a target, finishes under control, and remains available | Speed comes from a shorter path and a useful endpoint |

**PARTNER EXPLORATION:** In masks and gloves, one partner presents an upper opening. The cutter makes one controlled diagonal or horizontal action, holds the endpoint for one beat, then names what that position can do next: threaten, cover, redirect, or leave. Alternate every three cuts.
{: #partner-exploration}

**CROSSED-HAND FAMILY:** Test whether uncrossing can carry a short-edge or Zwerch-like action to a real new line without a withdrawal.
{: #crossed-hand-family}

**UNCROSSED-HAND FAMILY:** Test whether a modest hip and torso turn can cross or redirect the blade while preserving balance, cover, and a visible cutting plane.
{: #uncrossed-hand-family}

**MODERN COACHING PHRASE:** In HEMA, we cut at things, not through things. Use the phrase to remove unnecessary follow-through, not to excuse contact that has no cutting quality.
{: #modern-coaching-phrase}

## Move from one opening to another

**MEYER SOURCE STUDY \| 0:30-0:45**

Use the historical source to sharpen the problem while keeping the evidence separate from the specific mechanics this class is testing.

**MEYER, 1570:** As soon as you have touched, do not tarry any longer, but instead let it fly quickly away from one opening to another.

**WHAT THE SOURCE SUPPORTS:** After contact, Meyer expects the sword to move promptly from one opening to another rather than remain idle. He presents the Zwerch as especially useful inside and under the opponent's sword, where broad long cuts may be unavailable, and he describes actions supported by the whole body.
{: #what-the-source-supports}

**WHAT THE SOURCE DOES NOT SETTLE:** Meyer does not give us one universal compact-cutting method, one correct hip rotation, or a tournament-validity test for every modern ruleset. The passage does not justify throwing a memorized Zwerch whenever the hands cross.
{: #what-the-source-does-not-settle}

| HISTORICAL CLUE | CLASSROOM HYPOTHESIS |
| --- | --- |
| Move quickly opening to opening | Reuse the first cut's endpoint instead of withdrawing to rebuild the attack |
| Zwerch works in constrained inside lines | Crossing and uncrossing may create short credible routes when distance collapses |
| The body supports the action | Small hip and torso changes may redirect the blade without demanding a large swing |

**SOURCE NOTE:** Joachim Meyer, Gründtliche Beschreibung der Kunst des Fechtens (1570), Longsword, fol. I.55r; English translation by Rebecca L. R. Garber and transcription by Michael Chidester, via Wiktenauer. The Zwerch summary follows Meyer's 1570 longsword material.
{: #source-note}

## Cut and Recycle

**GAME 1 \| 0:45-1:05**

Make the first cut credible, experience its endpoint, and launch no more than one follow-up without withdrawing or making an oversized reset.

**WHY THIS GAME NOW:** The technical exploration was cooperative. This adds an honest defensive action while keeping the next line readable enough for students to discover a usable route.
{: #why-this-game-now}

**STARTING STATE:** Full gear. Begin at close striking measure. The defender presents either upper opening. The attacker makes a controlled credible cut. Once that cut has launched, the defender closes the attacked side and may leave the opposite upper line available.
{: #starting-state}

**ATTACKER:** Deliver the first cut at the target. If the cover creates a real second opening, attack it directly from the endpoint. If the route jams or the line is not present, cover or leave.
{: #attacker}

**DEFENDER:** Give an honest cover that stops the first action without becoming a theatrical sweep. Remain responsible after the cover so a weak follow-up can be answered.
{: #defender}

**STUDENT RULES:** Maximum two cuts. The first must matter; the second must come from where the first actually finished. Reset on a clean hit, double, loss of balance, weak contact, or a large withdrawal to restart the sword.
{: #student-rules}

**DOSE AND ROTATION:** Use 90-second roles, then switch. Change partners after both roles. No points.
{: #dose-and-rotation}

**COACH WATCHES:** Pausing after the first cut, preloading the second before the cover occurs, or shrinking the follow-up until it becomes a tap.
{: #coach-watches}

**IF THE ROOM IS READY:** Let the defender vary the timing and depth of the cover or decline to leave the expected second line.
{: #if-the-room-is-ready}

**IF THE ROOM NEEDS LESS:** Use one known cover and one known second opening until the endpoint can be recycled without a reset.
{: #if-the-room-needs-less}

## Cross and Uncross

**GAME 2 \| 1:05-1:25**

Use crossed or uncrossed hand positions as information, not as commands to complete a memorized combination.

**WHY THIS GAME NOW:** Game 1 established a clean recycle. Now the defender can produce different blade relationships, forcing the attacker to choose a Zwerch-like line change, a body-supported redirection, or an exit.
{: #why-this-game-now_1}

**STARTING STATE:** Full gear. The attacker begins with one controlled cut from close measure. The defender may cover from either side and may give light pressure, yield, or keep the line firm. Contact stays at drilling intensity.
{: #starting-state_1}

**ATTACKER:** If the hands are crossed and a line exists, test an uncrossing action. If the hands are uncrossed, test a modest hip and torso turn and the shortest supported route. If the defender owns the center or no target is open, cover and leave.
{: #attacker_1}

**DEFENDER:** Make one honest defense, remain capable of an immediate return, and answer a stalled or structurally weak second action with controlled contact. Do not feed a predetermined solution.
{: #defender_1}

**STUDENT RULES:** The attacker gets one possible follow-up, never a third cut, and must be able to name the opening that caused it. The defender may answer after the follow-up or as soon as the attacker stalls, forces through control, or withdraws to reload.
{: #student-rules_1}

**DOSE AND ROTATION:** Use 90-second roles and change partners after a full cycle. Keep each phrase short enough that the starting relationship remains visible.
{: #dose-and-rotation_1}

**COACH WATCHES:** Whether the second cut answers the defender's actual pressure and opening; whether the sword stays supported; and whether leaving remains available.
{: #coach-watches_1}

**IF THE ROOM IS READY:** Vary pressure, distance, and whether the expected opening appears.
{: #if-the-room-is-ready_1}

**IF THE ROOM NEEDS LESS:** Limit the defender to two named covers and slow the first exchange until the hand relationship is visible.
{: #if-the-room-needs-less_1}

## Cut, Change, or Leave

**GAME 3 \| 1:25-1:40**

Remove the promised follow-up. The attacker decides whether a second cut is actually better than recovering or leaving measure.

**WHY THIS GAME NOW:** The previous games taught a route. This game tests whether students can decline that route when the defender's cover, pressure, or distance makes it wrong.
{: #why-this-game-now_2}

**STARTING STATE:** Full gear. Begin just outside close striking measure. The designated attacker enters with one compact cut. After that action begins, both fencers may defend, reposition, and answer normally within the constraints.
{: #starting-state_2}

**ATTACKER:** Create one credible first threat. Then choose: finish the hit, make one immediate line change, or recover and leave.
{: #attacker_2}

**DEFENDER:** Use any safe defense or evasion. Answer immediately when the attacker repeats blindly, pauses, or loses cover.
{: #defender_2}

**STUDENT RULES:** The designated attacker is limited to two cuts before recovering or exiting. The phrase ends on a clean hit, a double, clear control, or full separation. Reset without debating points.
{: #student-rules_2}

**DOSE AND ROTATION:** Use 90-second roles and change partners after both roles. Change first cuts and starting guards so the endpoint cannot become a memorized pattern.
{: #dose-and-rotation_2}

**COACH WATCHES:** A recognizable second cut with no visible re-chamber; a decision that changes with the defender; and an exit that remains covered and balanced.
{: #coach-watches_2}

**IF THE ROOM IS READY:** Remove the designated attacker and let either fencer recognize the close-measure moment.
{: #if-the-room-is-ready_2}

**IF THE ROOM NEEDS LESS:** Return to one known first cut and two possible defensive answers: one that allows the follow-up and one that requires leaving.
{: #if-the-room-needs-less_2}

## Transfer without chasing the technique

**OPEN SPARRING \| 1:40-2:00**

Return to normal fencing. Compact follow-ups should appear only when measure and blade relationship make them useful.

**FORMAT:** Fence normal Steel Crown rounds of 3-4 minutes and rotate partners. Use the room's ordinary scoring and intensity agreements. Add no bonus points for compact cutting.
{: #format}

**SHARED OBJECTIVE:** Notice when distance closes and a conventional reset would be too slow. If a credible line change is present, use it. If not, defend, work within the club's current close-play rules, or separate safely.
{: #shared-objective}

**COACH OBSERVES:** Whether the first cut genuinely required a defense; whether the next action begins from the real endpoint; whether the second opening actually appeared; and whether the fencer can stop after one action, remain covered after two, and avoid the double.
{: #coach-observes}

**FEEDBACK RHYTHM:** Watch several phrases without interrupting. Between rounds, give one cue or ask one question. Do not turn live fencing into a hunt for the featured technique.
{: #feedback-rhythm}

**TRANSFER CUE:** When the space gets small: cut at the target, finish useful, and let the exchange decide whether you recycle or leave.
{: #transfer-cue}

**OPTIONAL CLOSE:** Ask: Which endpoint gave you the cleanest next action, and when should you have left instead? Take two fast answers and stop.
{: #optional-close}

## Observation and troubleshooting

**COACH REFERENCE**

Protect the distinction between efficient movement and meaningless contact. More cuts are not the outcome; better decisions from useful positions are.

| IF YOU SEE... | TRY... |
| --- | --- |
| Tiny wrist taps or flat contact | Restore a visible target and cutting plane; slow down until edge and body support return |
| Hands collapsed into the torso | Give the hands room to work and recover structure before asking for speed |
| Huge hip turn or exposed back | Reduce rotation and keep both the opponent and the exit available |
| Preset Zwerch chain | Let the defender decline the expected opening and require the attacker to name what changed |
| Second cut through firm control | Make leaving a successful answer; reward recognition rather than stubborn continuation |
| Frantic multi-cutting | Reinstate the two-cut limit and a one-beat endpoint check |

**QUESTIONS THAT PRESERVE DISCOVERY:** Did the first cut need to be defended? What actual opening or pressure caused the follow-up? Where did the first cut leave the hands, point, hips, and balance? Could the fencer stop, cover, or leave from that same position?
{: #questions-that-preserve-discovery}

**RECORD FOR THE NEXT CLASS:** Note which endpoints produced credible actions, which routes jammed, what measure made compact cutting necessary, and what contact standard students could reproduce. Let those findings shape Compact Cutting II rather than forcing the hypothesis to survive contrary evidence.
{: #record-for-the-next-class}

**ADAPTATIONS:** Newer fencers receive a fixed side and known cover. Advanced fencers vary pressure, rhythm, and whether the expected line appears. Odd groups use an observer who names the endpoint, the opening, and the final decision.
{: #adaptations}
