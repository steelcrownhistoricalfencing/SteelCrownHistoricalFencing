# Create Commitment I

[← All classes and lessons](index.md)

**Class context:** 120 MINUTES \| MEYER HISTORICAL LENS

## Create commitment through an honest threat

**LESSON OVERVIEW**

Students learn to make an opponent answer a credible first action, read the response, and choose the next action from the information they receive.

### CENTRAL QUESTION

How can I make an opponent reveal or commit to a response before I risk my decisive attack?

### END-OF-CLASS BEHAVIOR

Students make a first action that could land if ignored, notice the opponent's actual response, and either take the opening, continue safely, or recover without forcing a pre-planned second action.

### HISTORICAL ANCHOR

Meyer's Provoker, Taker, and Hitter framework: an action can draw an opponent out of advantage, manage the response that arrives, and exploit the opening the response creates.

### THE CUE

Make them answer. Then follow the answer.

**DESIGN CHOICE** The cognitive load stays low in the room: one problem, one cue, and one or two rules at a time. The plan itself stays detailed so the coach can recognize what is happening and decide what to change.

## What successful fencing looks like

**LEARNING OUTCOMES**

Look for a clear relationship between the first action, the opponent's response, and the fencer's next choice.

| LOOK FOR | WHAT IT MEANS |
| --- | --- |
| Honest first action | The first action could touch safely if the opponent ignores it; it is not a gesture made from too far away. |
| Attention | The student sees a real response before choosing the continuation. |
| Flexible choice | The student takes, continues, recovers, or leaves based on what appeared - not a memorized second target. |
| Survival | The student stays able to cover or abort; a double is feedback that the solution was not yet safe. |

### The lesson spine

### QUESTION

What did your first action make them do?

### EVIDENCE

I can describe the response before I claim the continuation was available.

### COMMON NON-EXAMPLE

I planned a second hit, noticed a small movement, and chased it even though it did not create access.

### WHAT THIS IS NOT

Not a requirement to feint every exchange, not a fixed two-hit combination, and not a contest to track points while the action is new.

## Introduce the tactical problem

**OPENING \| 0:00-0:05**

Frame the central question and give students a clear picture of the behavior they will explore.

### OPENING SENTENCE

"Tonight, your first action is allowed to be the thing that makes the next action possible. Make it real enough that they have to answer it - then let their answer tell you what comes next."

### SUCCESS DEFINITION

The first action is credible. The second choice follows evidence. The fencer can still cover, leave, or recover if the answer is not the one they expected.

### CLASS ARC

Experience the problem; compare what happened; study a historical model; add choice; then test the behavior in ordinary fencing.

**DO NOT** Teach the complete historical model before Game 1. Students should first possess the problem that the source helps explain.

## Prepare to enter, threaten, and recover

## Make Me Answer

**GAME 1 \| 0:15-0:35**

Explore the difference between a credible provocation and movement the opponent can ignore.

### WHY THIS GAME NOW

Before students learn labels or combinations, they need to feel when an action actually changes the opponent's options. The responder is allowed to act naturally so the information stays real.

### STARTING STATE

Both begin just outside measure. One fencer is the provoker; the other responds naturally. Begin slowly enough that both can make a clean choice.

### SAY TO STUDENTS

"Make one action that could touch if I ignored it. Then fence what actually happens. If there is no opening, you are allowed to recover."

### RULES

The first action must be capable of landing safely if ignored. After that, both fence the actual exchange. There is no point system, bonus target, or required second hit.

### ROTATION

90 seconds as provoker, 90 seconds as responder, then change partner after one complete A/B cycle. Repeat until the coach has seen enough examples. Keep the changes quick.

### COACH WATCHES

Threats from too far away; a second action chosen before evidence; oversized first actions; unsafe pursuit; and useful small cues being missed - head tilt, guard lift, shoulder turn, weight shift, a step, or stillness.

**IMPORTANT** Small movement is information, not an automatic target. Continue only when it produces a usable opening.

## Name the response and re-test the action

**GUIDED COACHING \| 0:35-0:50**

Use evidence from the first game to help students distinguish an empty gesture, an honest threat, and an unexpected answer.

### Ask before you explain

 What did they have to do because of your first action?  What did you actually see before you chose the next action?  What happened when they did not give the response you expected?

### Show three outcomes

### 1. EMPTY GESTURE

The opponent can hold position because it could not reach. No information was created.

### 2. HONEST THREAT

The opponent must parry, shift, withdraw, or counter. Now there is something to read.

### 3. DIFFERENT ANSWER

The opponent answers in an unexpected way. The attacker covers, recovers, or leaves rather than forcing a planned finish.

### ONE CUE FOR THE RE-TEST

Make them answer. Then follow the answer.

### FEEDBACK RHYTHM

Observe a few exchanges -&gt; name what happened -&gt; ask what they saw -&gt; give one cue -&gt; let the next round test it.

## Provoker, Taker, and Hitter

**MEYER SOURCE STUDY \| 0:50-1:05**

Read Meyer's language in context and connect the three tactical functions to the fencing problem students have experienced.

### Directly from the 1570 print

**ENGLISH - DUSACK 6** ...sometimes the first, now the second, then the third becomes a Provoker, Taker, or Hitter.

### ORIGINAL GERMAN

...das bißweilen der erst, jetzt der ander, dann der dritt ein Reitzer, Nemer, oder Treffer wirdt.

### COACHING INTERPRETATION

The first, second, or third cut can serve as the Provoker, Taker, or Hitter. Meyer is marking a cut's job in the exchange, not assigning it a permanent place in a three-beat sequence. [1]

### Related long-sword explanation

**ENGLISH - LUND 2.7** ...not because you desire to hit, but rather provoke him to strike out from his position of advantage...

### ORIGINAL GERMAN

...nit das du begerst zutreffenn, sonder ihn reitzest, auß seinem vortheil zuschlagen...

### COACHING INTERPRETATION

Enter with a high or low cut not simply to hit, but to draw the opponent out of advantage into a counteraction. Take that incoming action away; the taking can expose them; then strike the opening. [2]

### TERMINOLOGY NOTE

The original terms are Reitzer, Nemer, and Treffer. This plan uses the common English labels Provoker, Taker, and Hitter. Translations and spellings vary by edition.

## Applying the three functions

**MEYER TACTICAL MODEL**

Use the historical text to sharpen attention to the opponent and clarify the tactical purpose of each action.

### PROVOKER

A credible action that changes the opponent's situation. It may be a cut, threat, invitation, or pressure, but it must be real enough that ignoring it has a consequence.

### TAKER

The work that manages the response that comes back: take it away, close it, redirect it, or otherwise keep it from deciding the exchange against you.

### HITTER

The action to the opening that the exchange actually produced. It is not owed to the fencer; if the opening did not appear, recover or leave.

### Three demonstration cases

### DIRECT ATTACK IS THERE

Hit directly. Do not manufacture a provocation when the line is already open.

### OPPONENT DEFENDS HARD

Give an honest high threat. If they rise or turn to defend, manage their blade or movement and take the line they created.

### OPPONENT EXITS OR COUNTERATTACKS

Do not force the Hitter. Cover, recover, or leave. The exchange still gave you valid information.

**SAY TO STUDENTS** "Threaten them with a real action. Let their defense make your next choice clearer. If it does not, leave safely and try again later."

## Read the answer and choose the continuation

**PROGRESSIVE GAMES \| 1:05-1:30**

The next two games add response variety and freer initiative while keeping the tactical problem clear.

### Game 2 - Three Honest Answers \| 12 minutes

### WHY THIS GAME NOW

The attacker must read a real answer instead of assuming every threat earns the same continuation.

### SETUP

The defender privately chooses to hold, parry/cover, or withdraw. The attacker begins outside measure with one credible high-line threat.

### SAY TO STUDENTS

"Make them answer. Then tell me which answer you saw before you take, continue, or recover."

### RULES

One honest threat. One defender answer. One honest continuation or recovery. Reset. No announced answer and no points.

### ROTATION

90 seconds each role; then change partner. Increase speed only when both fencers can identify the response without guessing.

### Game 3 - Provoke From the Onset \| 13 minutes

### WHY THIS GAME NOW

Provocation now competes with direct attack and safe disengagement. It should become a useful option, not a behavior students force every exchange.

### SETUP AND RULE

Free onset from outside measure. Either fencer may attack directly, provoke, or leave. If someone chooses a provocation, its first action must be honest and the continuation must follow the opponent's actual response.

### ROTATION

Three-minute rounds; then switch partners. Save feedback for the breaks unless an immediate safety correction is necessary.

## Transfer the behavior to ordinary fencing

**OPEN SPARRING \| 1:30-1:55**

Students fence freely and use provocation selectively when it helps them create and recognize an opening.

### FORMAT

Five four-minute rounds with short rotation and water breaks. Use normal Steel Crown intensity agreements and pair by compatible control and size.

### SHARED OBJECTIVE

"Fence normally. When the direct attack is uncertain, make them answer; then follow the answer. If they give you nothing, do not invent an opening."

### COACH BEHAVIOR

Watch several exchanges before speaking. Give individual feedback between rounds, not a checklist students must carry while fencing.

### TRANSFER EVIDENCE

A student uses provocation selectively, sees the answer before continuing, and can decline the continuation when the exchange is no longer safe.

**FINISH \| 1:55-2:00** No formal reflection is required. Use the time for gear transition and individual feedback. If a whole-group close helps, ask only: 'What did you see that changed your choice?'

## Adjust difficulty while preserving the problem

**SAFETY AND ADAPTATION**

Use these options to match uncertainty, speed, distance, and impact to the needs of the room.

| CONDITION | PLANNED RESPONSE |
| --- | --- |
| First-time or overwhelmed student | Use a known attack or response, longer preparation time, controlled partner, and safe recovery as success. |
| Mixed experience | Give newer fencers less uncertainty; give experienced fencers more timing or rhythm variation, not greater impact. |
| Odd attendance | Use attacker, defender, and observer. Observer names the first action and response, then roles rotate. |
| Limited space | Use narrow lanes; prohibit passing and large leaps; emphasize compact footwork and controlled stops. |
| Injury or low-impact day | Replace bounds with steps; reduce speed and contact; permit limited-role or observation participation. |
| Game breaks down | Remove one variable, increase distance, slow the action, or return to a known starting state. |
| Time loss | Compress explanation and one role interval; preserve open transfer and a calm finish. |

## Adjust the task when learning stalls

**COACH TROUBLESHOOTING**

Use the pattern you observe to choose one practical adjustment, then return students to the task.

| IF YOU SEE... | DO THIS |
| --- | --- |
| Students freeze or overthink | Return to one honest threat and two defender answers. Ask only: 'What did they do?' |
| Students pre-plan the second hit | Have the defender change response more clearly. The attacker may recover instead of forcing a finish. |
| The first action is decorative | Increase distance clarity; make the defender ignore it until it could genuinely land. |
| Students chase tiny motions | Remind them: a head tilt or guard shift is information, not an automatic target. Continue only if it creates usable access. |
| The room is overloaded | Remove a rule, remove a choice, and remove all scorekeeping. Keep the opponent and the honest threat. |

**CLASS STANDARD** For new material, do not combine a new action, several rules, and scorekeeping. Keep the opponent information real, then add complexity only after students can attend to it.

## What to watch during live fencing

**OBSERVATION AND FEEDBACK**

Use these cues to recognize progress, diagnose common failures, and give focused feedback between rounds.

| DOMAIN | LOOK FOR | COMMON FAILURE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Credibility | First action can land and demands respect | Decorative movement outside measure |
| Perception | Waits for and names the response | Second action begins before information |
| Structure | Can take, cover, or abort | Fake leaves hands or body exposed |
| Choice | Target follows created opening | Always attacks the same second target |
| Timing | Redirects during the opponent's response | Pauses after a successful provocation |
| Transfer | Uses provocation selectively | Tries to feint every exchange |

### Between-round feedback formula

Observation -&gt; question -&gt; one cue -&gt; re-test. Name what happened, ask what information the student perceived, offer one change, and let the next round test it.

### CLASS-LEVEL SUCCESS EVIDENCE

Cleaner solutions and fewer doubles from Game 1 to Game 3; students can name a reliable cue; and the target behavior appears selectively in open fencing.

## Make Me Answer

**GAME CARD 1**

Expose the difference between a credible provocation and an empty feint.

### STARTING STATE

Both begin just outside measure. One is the provoker; the other responds naturally.

### SAY TO STUDENTS

"Make one action that could touch if I ignored it. Then fence what actually happens. If there is no opening, recover."

### DOSE AND ROTATION

90 seconds per role. Switch roles immediately; change partner after a complete A/B cycle.

### COACH WATCHES

Distance, credibility, pre-selected second targets, unsafe pursuit, and missed information.

### IF THE ROOM IS READY

Allow the responder to give smaller or more varied responses while the attacker remains responsible for proving that an opening exists.

### IF THE ROOM NEEDS LESS

Use a known opening and one or two clear defender responses. Slow the exchange.

## Three Honest Answers

**GAME CARD 2**

Force the attacker to read rather than assume the opponent's reaction.

### STARTING STATE

The defender privately chooses hold, parry/cover, or withdrawal before the exchange. The attacker begins outside measure.

### SAY TO STUDENTS

"Make them answer. Before you continue, know which answer they gave you."

### DOSE AND ROTATION

90 seconds per role; then change partner. Keep speed controlled until the response is readable.

### COACH WATCHES

Whether the attacker waits for evidence, whether the first action remains safe, and whether recovery is accepted as a valid choice.

### IF THE ROOM IS READY

Let the defender choose during the exchange or offer a smaller partial response.

### IF THE ROOM NEEDS LESS

Use only hold or full parry. Increase distance and slow the action.

## Provoke From the Onset

**GAME CARD 3**

Make provocation compete with direct attack and safe disengagement in a freer context.

### STARTING STATE

Free onset from outside measure. Either fencer may choose to attack directly, provoke, or leave.

### SAY TO STUDENTS

"Fence normally. If you provoke, make it honest and let their response choose your continuation."

### DOSE AND ROTATION

Three-minute rounds, then change partner. Coach between rounds rather than after every exchange.

### COACH WATCHES

Selective use, credibility, reading, safe abortion, and whether opponent adaptation changes the choice.

### IF THE ROOM IS READY

Remove the designated initiator and allow either fencer to begin when the room is ready.

### IF THE ROOM NEEDS LESS

Return to a fixed initial threat and two defender choices.
