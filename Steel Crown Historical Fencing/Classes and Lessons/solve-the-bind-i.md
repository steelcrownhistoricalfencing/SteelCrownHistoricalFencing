# Solve the Bind I

[← All classes and lessons](index.md)

**Class context:** 120 MINUTES \| LIECHTENAUER TRADITION LENS

## The problem, behavior, and boundaries

The class introduces one binary information problem - hard or soft pressure - and two broad responses. It does not attempt to teach the complete winding system.

**CENTRAL QUESTION:** When the swords meet and remain in contact, how can I use pressure information to choose a safe continuation?
{: #central-question}

**END-OF-CLASS BEHAVIOR:** Students remain calm at contact, feel whether the opponent is maintaining firm pressure or yielding, and choose a continuation that exploits that relationship without looking down or forcing a memorized technique.
{: #end-of-class-behavior}

**HISTORICAL ANCHOR:** The Pseudo-Peter von Danzig gloss of Liechtenauer on Feeling, Indes, and working according to soft or hard pressure in the bind.
{: #historical-anchor}

## Target-action success test

The target behavior counts only when all four observable conditions are present.

| REQUIREMENT | OBSERVABLE STANDARD |
| --- | --- |
| Contact | The student maintains useful blade contact long enough to receive information without freezing. |
| Feeling | The student distinguishes sustained firm pressure from yielding or light contact. |
| Choice | Against firm pressure, the student redirects or leaves the pressure; against soft contact, the student controls through to the opening. |
| Protection | The continuation keeps the opponent's threat contained and does not become a strength contest or double. |

**NO DOUBLE:** A result that leaves both fencers validly hit does not satisfy the lesson, even if one blade arrived first.

### Primary functional model

At the instant of contact, keep the hands functional and point threatening. If the opponent presses firmly, do not push harder; redirect or leave. If the opponent yields, maintain controlled pressure toward the nearest opening.

### Instructor hypothesis

Students will decide the technique before contact, stare at the blades, push against strength, or interpret any initial collision as a stable hard bind.

## Arrival and orientation

**C \| BLOCK 1 \| 0:00-0:05**

Students understand the problem, success test, and class arc.

**OPENING SENTENCE:** "Tonight we are solving one problem: When the swords meet and remain in contact, how can I use pressure information to choose a safe continuation?"
{: #opening-sentence}

**SUCCESS DEFINITION:** State the four-part success test in plain language and make clear that a double is a failed solution.
{: #success-definition}

**CLASS ARC:** Experience the problem first; compare what worked; examine one historical lens; increase choice and pressure; test transfer in open fencing.
{: #class-arc}

**DO NOT:** Teach the complete historical solution before the first game. Students should possess the problem before receiving the model.

## Athletic development

**C \| BLOCK 2 \| 0:05-0:20**

Maintain postural control while receiving variable partner pressure and redirecting it without stiffening.

**WHAT THIS BLOCK IS:** A short movement lesson, not conditioning. Demonstrate each exercise once, keep the volume low, and stop or regress when posture or landing quality deteriorates.
{: #what-this-block-is}

**TARGET QUALITY:** Maintain postural control while receiving variable partner pressure and redirecting it without stiffening.
{: #target-quality}

### Exercise sequence

| EXERCISE | DOSE | COACHING FOCUS | MOVEMENT PURPOSE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grip-pressure scale | 2 x 20 seconds | Use 2-4 of 10 grip; shoulders relaxed | Separate sensitivity from excessive gripping |
| Partner palm pressure | 2 x 30 seconds each | Feel changes through contact; do not anticipate | Introduce hard/soft information without swords |
| Split-stance anti-rotation hold | 2 x 15 seconds each side | Ribs over hips; breathe; feet quiet | Maintain structure under lateral pressure |
| Press-or-yield step reaction | 3 x 20 seconds each | Follow yield; redirect pressure; no tugging | Connect tactile cue to an immediate directional choice |

### How to perform the exercises

### 1. GRIP-PRESSURE SCALE

Hold the sword only if the room is ready; otherwise use an imaginary handle. Squeeze as hard as possible for one second to identify 10 of 10, release fully to 0, then settle at 2-4. Open and close the fingers without changing wrist position. Cue: 'Secure enough to transmit information, relaxed enough to receive it.'

### 2. PARTNER PALM PRESSURE

Partners touch one palm at chest height. The leader alternates between gently pressing forward and softening or withdrawing the hand. The follower keeps contact without leaning and takes one small step: redirect diagonally when pressure grows, follow forward when it softens. Change slowly and unpredictably. Cue: 'Do not guess; let pressure tell you.'

### 3. SPLIT-STANCE ANTI-ROTATION HOLD

Stand in fencing stance. Partner places one open hand lightly on the student's forearm or shoulder and applies gentle sideways pressure for three seconds. Student stays tall, breathes, and resists only enough to keep posture. No sudden shoves. Switch sides. Cue: 'Structure, not stiffness.'

### 4. PRESS-OR-YIELD STEP REACTION

Partners connect both palms or padded forearms. Leader either increases steady pressure or softly removes it. On pressure, follower takes a short diagonal step and guides the contact past; on yield, follower takes one controlled forward step while maintaining contact. Reset after each choice. Cue: 'Pressure: redirect. Yield: follow.'

**REGRESSION AND STOP RULE:** Reduce speed, distance, or impact; replace jumps with controlled steps. Stop for pain, dizziness, repeated loss of balance, loud uncontrolled landings, or a student who cannot maintain the described posture.
{: #regression-and-stop-rule}

## Representative problem exposure: Hard or Soft

**C \| BLOCK 3 \| 0:20-0:40**

Make tactile information determine the continuation.

**STARTING STATE:** Begin in a safe crossed-blade position at medium distance, points off-line. One partner secretly chooses firm pressure or soft/yielding contact.
{: #starting-state}

**PLAYER GOALS:** Solver identifies the pressure through contact and makes the matching controlled action: redirect around firm pressure or maintain control through soft contact.
{: #player-goals}

**RULES AND CONSTRAINTS:** No initial strike; action begins on a neutral cue; slow speed; halt after one continuation.
{: #rules-and-constraints}

**SCORING:** Correct read and protected touch 2; correct read without touch 1; wrong-direction force or double 0.
{: #scoring}

**DOSE AND ROTATION:** 4 minutes each role; change partners and repeat from the opposite bind.
{: #dose-and-rotation}

**INSTRUCTOR OBSERVES:** Pre-planning, staring down, grip tension, pushing harder, loss of point threat, and confusing impact with sustained pressure.
{: #instructor-observes}

**COACH POSTURE:** Observe across the room before intervening. Record common patterns and let several exchanges occur before deciding what the class needs.

## Guided coaching

**C \| BLOCK 4 \| 0:40-0:55**

Use the first game's evidence to provide the smallest intervention that improves the class.

### Debrief questions

1. What did firm pressure feel like in your hands?

2. What changed when the opponent yielded?

3. When did you start making the decision?

4. How did looking at the opponent rather than the blades affect your response?

**PRIMARY INTERVENTION:** Demonstrate equal pushing, abandoning contact blindly, and two functional responses. Cue: 'Feel first: pressure redirects you; absence lets you go through.' Clarify that hard/soft is relational and can change.
{: #primary-intervention}

**VARIABLE REHEARSAL:** Partners re-test the target problem at approximately 60-70% intensity. Keep at least one meaningful variable - side, rhythm, distance, pressure, or response - rather than feeding identical repetitions.
{: #variable-rehearsal}

**FEEDBACK PLAN:** Use one cue at a time. Correct immediate danger at once; otherwise give feedback between sets of three attempts. Ask the student to identify the information that shaped the action.
{: #feedback-plan}

**AVOID:** Turning the block into a long lecture or adding several fixes before students can test the first one.

## Historical integration: Liechtenauer Tradition Lens

**C \| BLOCK 5 \| 0:55-1:05**

Connect the experienced tactical problem to a documented historical principle without claiming that the source prescribes the modern game.

**SOURCE IDENTIFICATION:** The Pseudo-Peter von Danzig gloss of Liechtenauer on Feeling, Indes, and working according to soft or hard pressure in the bind.
{: #source-identification}

**VERIFIED HISTORICAL CLAIM:** The Pseudo-Peter von Danzig gloss instructs the fencer to feel whether the opponent is soft or hard as the swords meet and then work Indes according to that perception, toward the nearest opening. It explicitly warns against hasty winding before the pressure is perceived. [1]
{: #verified-historical-claim}

**MODERN TRAINING INTERPRETATION:** From a right-side bind, if the partner remains firm, keep cover and redirect around or disengage to the available opening. If the partner yields, maintain controlled blade relationship and advance the point. These are introductory functional categories, not a claim that every bind has only two techniques.
{: #modern-training-interpretation}

**DEMONSTRATION:** Show three slow examples: one successful application, one common failure, and one case in which the action should be declined. Let students rehearse briefly, then return to live choice.
{: #demonstration}

**EVIDENCE LINE:** Historical text supplies the cited principle or action. The success test, games, scoring, cues, and safety adaptations are modern coaching design.

## Progressive constraints

**C \| BLOCK 6 \| 1:05-1:30**

Increase uncertainty while preserving the lesson's central information-action relationship.

### Game 2 - Changing Pressure \| 12 minutes

Prevent students from treating hard and soft as fixed partner identities.

- Begin from blade contact created by a controlled cut and parry. Pressure leader may remain firm, yield, or change once.

- Solver follows current information and reaches a safe opening without losing control.
- One pressure change maximum; no grappling; halt on clean touch, double, or broken contact with safe separation.

- Correct adaptation and clean touch 2; safe disengagement 1; leader clean hit 1; double 0.
- 5 minutes each role; 2 minutes transition.

### Game 3 - Earn the Bind \| 13 minutes

Connect tactile decision-making to the exchange that creates contact.

- Free onset; one fencer initiates a controlled cut that the other may meet, avoid, or counter.
- If sustained contact occurs, either fencer may solve it using pressure information. No one is required to remain in a poor bind.

- Normal movement; no grappling; continue to clean score, double, or safe separation.
- Clean score after correct bind decision 2; any other clean score 1; double 0.
- Change initiator every 3 minutes; final minute reset.

**PROGRESSION TRIGGER:** Open fencing with no designated initiator.
{: #progression-trigger}

**REGRESSION TRIGGER:** Require one known cut and parry to create the same bind.
{: #regression-trigger}

## Open sparring

**C \| BLOCK 7 \| 1:30-1:55**

Test whether the behavior appears without forced actions or bonus scoring.

**FORMAT:** Five 4-minute rounds with approximately 1 minute between rounds for rotation and brief feedback. Use normal open-fencing rules and the club's usual intensity agreement.
{: #format}

**SHARED OBJECTIVE:** "Fence normally. If meaningful blade contact persists, resist the urge to force a favorite action: feel the present pressure, choose, and keep the opponent's threat contained."
{: #shared-objective}

**PAIRING PLAN:** Use compatible control and size, then rotate across different rhythms and preferences. Supervise novice pairings and avoid a large mismatch solely to complete the rotation.
{: #pairing-plan}

### Coach records

- Looks at opponent while receiving blade information
- Maintains useful, non-rigid connection
- Distinguishes sustained hard/soft pressure
- Updates continuation from pressure
- Point and hilt remain protective
- Reaches opening without strength contest
- Uses feeling only when bind persists

**FEEDBACK:** Observe during rounds and coach mainly between them. Open sparring is not another constrained game.

## Reflection and close

**C \| BLOCK 8 \| 1:55-2:00**

Students consolidate one usable cue and one testable next action.

### Whole-group questions

1. What tactile cue separated firm from yielding contact?

2. When did redirecting work better than pushing?

3. When was leaving the bind safer than staying?

**INDIVIDUAL NEXT ACTION:** Each student states one small, observable action they will test the next time this problem appears.
{: #individual-next-action}

**INSTRUCTOR CLOSE:** Restate the functional success test, not a technique name. Record any safety issue, equipment problem, or student who needs a lower-intensity follow-up.
{: #instructor-close}

**END ON TIME:** Do not let the final sparring round consume reflection. The class is incomplete until students name the information that shaped their decision.

## Plan modifications before the room forces them

Preserve the information and decision while reducing uncertainty, speed, distance, or impact.

| CONDITION | PLANNED RESPONSE |
| --- | --- |
| First-time student | Use a known attack or response, longer preparation time, controlled partner, and score safe resolution as success. |
| Mixed experience | Give beginners less uncertainty; give experienced students more choice or rhythm variation, not greater impact. |
| Odd attendance | Use attacker, defender, and observer. Observer applies the four-part success test, then roles rotate. |
| Limited space | Use narrow lanes; prohibit passing and large leaps; emphasize compact footwork and controlled stops. |
| Injury / low impact | Replace bounds with steps; reduce speed and contact; permit observer or limited-role participation. |
| Large attendance | Create lanes, assign a senior student to each cluster, and synchronize changes with one timer. |
| Game breaks down | Remove one variable, increase distance, slow the action, or return to a known starting state. |
| Time loss | Compress explanation and one role interval; preserve open transfer and reflection. |

## What to watch without interrupting every exchange

Use the checklist to distinguish learning from mere scoring.

| DOMAIN | LOOK FOR | COMMON FAILURE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attention | Looks at opponent while receiving blade information | Stares down at crossing |
| Contact | Maintains useful, non-rigid connection | Loses blade or clamps down |
| Feeling | Distinguishes sustained hard/soft pressure | Reacts only to collision |
| Choice | Updates continuation from pressure | Runs memorized technique |
| Structure | Point and hilt remain protective | Hands collapse or point wanders |
| Outcome | Reaches opening without strength contest | Pushes harder or doubles |
| Transfer | Uses feeling only when bind persists | Manufactures static binds |

### Between-round feedback formula

Observation -&gt; question -&gt; one cue -&gt; re-test. Name what happened, ask what information the student perceived, offer one change, and let the next round test it.

### Class-level success evidence

- Cleaner solutions and fewer doubles from Game 1 to Game 3.
- Students can name at least one reliable cue.
- The target behavior appears selectively in open fencing.
- Students can explain why an unsafe score does not satisfy the lesson.
- Beginners show safer structure even when they do not score.
